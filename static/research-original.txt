My balcony garden plan

I am planning to grow herbs on my balcony this spring because I would like to have fresh ingredients available when I cook. The balcony receives direct sunlight in the morning, but the neighboring building shades it during the afternoon. I have not measured the exact amount of sunlight yet, so I need to do that before choosing all the plants. My initial preference is for basil, mint, and parsley, although I may change this list after learning more about their needs.

The space is limited, and I do not want the pots to make it difficult to open the balcony door. I intend to start with 3 containers rather than filling the entire area immediately. This should allow me to learn how much maintenance is required before making a larger commitment. I also need to check that the containers have drainage holes and that any water underneath them will not cause problems for my downstairs neighbor.

My budget for the first stage is $60. That amount needs to cover the containers, potting mix, and the plants themselves. I already own a small watering can, so I do not expect to purchase another one. If the total cost is higher than expected, I will reduce the number of plants rather than increase the budget. I want this to remain a manageable experiment instead of becoming an expensive project.

Watering is the part I am most uncertain about. I sometimes travel for work, and I cannot assume that a neighbor will always be available to help. Before buying anything, I will consider what would happen if I were away for 4 days. A simple self-watering container might help, but I have not compared prices or checked whether it would be suitable for the plants I want.

I hope to begin on 18 April, provided that the weather is suitable and I have finished the necessary checks. After the first month, I will review which plants are doing well and whether I am enjoying the routine. If the experiment is successful, I may add another herb later. For now, my goal is simply to learn and grow a small amount of food without taking on more than I can comfortably maintain.
